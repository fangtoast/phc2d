\section{Results}

The results follow the evaluation sequence: forward prediction (RQ1), query
generalization (RQ2), and MPB-replayed inverse design (RQ3). Each subsection
reports the primary finding, its quantitative support, and its evidence scope.

\subsection{BISON Reduces Full-Band Prediction Error}

Across three seeds, BISON achieved the lowest full-band test error among the
four evaluated models (Table~\ref{tab:forward}). Its MRE was
\(1.0299\%\pm0.0545\%\), compared with
\(1.3309\%\pm0.0122\%\) for ResMLP-wide, the strongest non-operator
baseline. This corresponds to a 22.6\% relative reduction. The lifted CNN and
plain MLP yielded 2.2234\% and 2.4764\%, respectively. BISON used 389,718
parameters, whereas each MLP model used 878,646.

Per-band MRE ranged from 0.9028\% for band 4 to 1.2765\% for band 10.
The error reduction therefore was not confined to one output band. BISON
required more training and inference time than the dense baselines, although
its mean inference latency remained below one millisecond per sample. Within
the fixed dataset and metric, RQ1 supports BISON over the three tested
baselines. The comparison neither attributes the gain to a specific BandBlock
component nor evaluates transfer across resolutions or lattices.

\subsection{Query Accuracy Degrades under Path Extrapolation}

The FNO query model achieved \(1.1004\%\pm0.0218\%\) MRE on unseen structures
over all sampled wave vectors and \(1.0470\%\pm0.0240\%\) on withheld
interpolation points. On the held-out path segment, its MRE increased to
\(3.6747\%\pm0.1730\%\). The band-order violation rate also increased from
2.16\% under interpolation to 9.22\%. Physical coordinates therefore enabled
flexible querying but did not ensure reliable extrapolation.

DeepONet had lower ordering-violation rates despite higher MRE, confirming that
pointwise accuracy and spectral ordering are distinct. RQ2 therefore supports
the FNO query model for interpolation, while identifying held-segment
extrapolation as a failure mode. We retain the query model as a diagnostic for
cross-surrogate disagreement, not as a second physical evaluator.

\subsection{Routing-Free Inverse Design after MPB Replay}

Across all 625 test targets, the single-mode generator achieved 2.1434\%
BISON MRE and 2.2614\% real-MPB MRE (Table~\ref{tab:inverse}). The
surrogate--replay gap was 0.118 percentage points. The replay distribution had
a median of 2.2196\%, a 10th percentile of 1.7264\%, and a 90th percentile of
2.8262\%. The 95\% bootstrap interval for the mean was [2.2263, 2.2965]\%.

Under the identical candidate and solver budget, the four-mode embedding
baseline achieved 2.3916\% MPB MRE. The paired difference (four-mode minus
single-mode) was 0.1302 percentage points, with bootstrap interval [0.0905,
0.1705] and two-sided sign test \(p=0.0010\). Thus, the single-mode variant had
lower per-target replay error for this frozen seed-0 comparison. The statistic
does not measure reproducibility across retraining or establish a general
architectural advantage.

RQ3 therefore supports the routing-free variant within the complete
625-target, seed-0 protocol. BISON ranked both candidate pools, and each target
received the same five MPB replays. The nonzero surrogate--replay gap confirms
that the learned ranking and physical evaluation remain distinct.

\subsection{Runtime and Evidence Scope}

Generating single-mode candidates for all 625 targets required 1.408~s, or
2.25~ms per target. BISON ranking required 221.333~s in total, or 354.13~ms per
target. Binary deduplication left 112.02 candidates per target on average. The
query audit yielded 2.1254\% MRE and differed from BISON by
0.8457 percentage points under its diagnostic definition. This disagreement
is reported but does not alter candidate ranking.

The forward comparison uses three seeds. The inverse comparison covers the
complete test set but uses only seed 0. Earlier retrieval and direct-inverse
results, field/PINO residuals, and historical seed-2 comparisons use different
protocols and are excluded from the current inverse evidence.

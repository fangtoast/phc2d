\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Ethics}

The three-seed forward comparison suggests that Fourier-space coupling and
joint band prediction are effective inductive biases for this fixed periodic
task. BISON achieved lower MRE than the three baselines and used fewer
parameters than the two MLPs, although its training and inference costs were
higher. Because no component ablation was performed, this evidence applies to
the complete BandBlock and not to any individual branch.

Forward accuracy alone did not determine inverse performance. BISON provided
the frozen objective and primary ranking, while the query operator measured
consistency in a coordinate-conditioned output space. The query
score did not modify the ranking. MPB replay then measured the selected
structures with the governing eigensolver. Reporting these quantities
separately exposes surrogate disagreement and the allocation of the physical
evaluation budget.

The inverse evidence is limited to one training seed. Its paired statistics
measure consistency across targets for that frozen run, not reproducibility
across retraining. The design space is also restricted to one binary,
fixed-material, \(\pgroup\)-symmetric family at a single resolution and along
one wave-vector path. Transfer to other lattices, materials, dimensions,
polarizations, and fabrication constraints was not evaluated. In addition,
the query experiment showed weak held-path extrapolation.

These limitations motivate three direct extensions. Component-wise ablations
should remove the second spectral kernel, gate, and low-rank branch under the
same three-seed forward protocol. A multi-seed inverse study should retrain both
generator variants and repeat the complete 625-target replay with identical
candidate hashes and solver budgets. Transfer studies should preserve
schema-compatible comparisons, while fabrication-aware evaluation should add
minimum-feature and robustness constraints before experimental claims.

The dataset is simulated and contains no personal or human-subject data. The
principal broader risk is misplaced confidence in the learned ranking, which
could waste solver, fabrication, or laboratory resources. Separate reporting
of surrogate and replay errors, held-path extrapolation, and difficult examples
reduces this risk. A candidate is treated as physically evaluated only after
MPB replay.

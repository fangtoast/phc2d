\section{Introduction}

Two-dimensional photonic crystals control electromagnetic propagation through
the geometry of a periodic unit cell. Periodicity organizes electromagnetic
states into bands and can suppress propagation over selected frequency
intervals~\cite{yablonovitch1987inhibited,john1987localization,
joannopoulos2008photonic}. The dispersion relation comprises ordered
eigenfrequencies along a path in the Brillouin zone. It determines the
location and width of band gaps, neighboring-band curvature, and slow-light
behavior. Unit-cell design is therefore often a full-band problem rather than
the prediction of a single scalar property.

Given a structure, a Maxwell eigensolver such as MIT Photonic Bands (MPB)
computes its band structure~\cite{johnson2001mpb}. The inverse problem instead
seeks a structure that realizes a prescribed dispersion. Topology optimization
has produced low-loss and fabrication-aware photonic devices
~\cite{jensen2004systematic,jensen2011topology,piggott2015demultiplexer,
piggott2017fabrication}, but repeated forward and adjoint solves remain costly
when many targets or candidates must be evaluated. Learned forward models can
reduce this online cost~\cite{peurifoy2018nanophotonic,
tahersima2019splitters}, although they introduce two coupled risks. The inverse
map is one-to-many, and search can exploit surrogate error. Tandem networks
address non-uniqueness by training through a frozen forward model
~\cite{liu2018tandem}; budget-aware benchmarks further show that inverse
methods must be compared under controlled search costs~\cite{ren2020benchmarking}.
Accordingly, an inverse evaluation must report both candidate and physical
replay budgets.

Neural operators provide a natural representation for the forward map because
they learn mappings between discretized functions. Fourier neural operators
(FNOs) parameterize global interactions through truncated Fourier modes
~\cite{li2021fno,kovachki2023neuraloperator}. Such global frequency-domain
mixing is well matched to a periodic input field. Photonic neural operators
have mainly predicted optical fields or device responses
~\cite{gu2022neurolight,zhu2024pace,augenstein2023emno}. Jointly predicting an
ordered eigenfrequency matrix and using that prediction in a controlled
inverse-design loop require a different output structure and evidence chain.

We introduce \method{}, short for \textbf{B}and-\textbf{I}nformed
\textbf{S}pectral \textbf{O}perator \textbf{N}etwork. The name refers to the
implemented \texttt{fno2d\_\allowbreak band\_\allowbreak v2} model. BISON
is a project-specific, FNO-inspired spectral surrogate for joint full-band
prediction. It maps a periodic permittivity grid to the complete sampled band
matrix and serves as the frozen objective and primary candidate ranker. A
single-mode routing-free latent generator proposes multiple structures for
each target. An independently trained wave-vector query operator provides a
cross-surrogate audit, and MPB evaluates only the fixed Top-5 subset. Thus,
learned models guide the search, while replayed MPB bands determine the reported
physical inverse result.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We develop BISON, a compact spectral architecture for complete
  photonic band matrices. Its BandBlock combines two-scale Fourier mixing with
  gated local, low-rank global, and residual updates.
  \item We embed frozen BISON in a budget-controlled inverse workflow with a
  single-mode routing-free generator, an independent query-model audit, and
  per-target MPB replay.
  \item We evaluate the evidence chain on fixed data partitions and budgets.
  The study includes a formal three-seed forward comparison on 10,000
  structures, query interpolation and extrapolation tests, and seed-0 replay
  over all 625 test targets. BISON reaches
  \(1.0299\%\pm0.0545\%\) forward MRE, and the inverse workflow reaches
  \(2.2614\%\) real-MPB MRE. Cross-seed inverse stability is not assessed.
\end{itemize}

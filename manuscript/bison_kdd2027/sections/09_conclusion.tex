\section{Conclusion}

We presented BISON, a band-informed FNO-derived network for predicting complete
two-dimensional photonic-crystal band matrices and guiding inverse candidate
selection. Its dual spectral kernels, channel gate, low-rank global branch,
and residual updates reduce three-seed forward MRE to
\(1.0299\%\pm0.0545\%\). Coupled to a single-mode latent generator, BISON
ranks 128 candidates per target before fixed-budget Top-5 MPB replay. The
complete seed-0 test split reaches 2.2614\% real-MPB MRE and outperforms the
same decoder with four mode embeddings by 0.1302 percentage points. Just as
importantly, query extrapolation and per-target replay expose the remaining
failure modes. BISON is therefore best viewed as an efficient neural operator
inside a solver-verified workflow, not as a replacement for the physical
eigensolver. More broadly, the study argues for a specific standard in learned
photonic inverse design: define the learned objective, expose the candidate
budget, retain an independent diagnostic where possible, and reserve physical
claims for candidates recomputed by the governing numerical solver.

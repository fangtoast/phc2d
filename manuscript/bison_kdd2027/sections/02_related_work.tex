\section{Related Work}

\subsection{From Solver-Based Photonic Design to Learned Search}

Numerical photonic design has a mature physical foundation. MPB solves the
frequency-domain Maxwell eigenproblem in a plane-wave basis
~\cite{johnson2001mpb}, while topology optimization exposes many material
degrees of freedom to gradient-based search~\cite{molesky2018inverse}. Early photonic-crystal work
demonstrated systematic low-loss bends~\cite{jensen2004systematic}; subsequent
work extended the framework to nano-photonic components and dispersion-shaped,
manufacturing-robust waveguides~\cite{jensen2011topology,wang2011robust}.
Inverse-designed grating couplers, wavelength demultiplexers, and explicitly
fabrication-constrained devices further established the practical value of
solver-coupled optimization~\cite{piggott2014grating,piggott2015demultiplexer,
piggott2017fabrication}. These methods retain physical fidelity during search,
but each new objective can require many expensive solves.

Data-driven methods move part of that cost into an offline dataset. Learned
forward models have accelerated nanoparticle simulation, power-splitter design,
and exploration of global component design spaces
~\cite{peurifoy2018nanophotonic,tahersima2019splitters,melati2019mapping}.
Tandem networks train an inverse map through a frozen forward surrogate to
reduce geometry averaging under non-uniqueness~\cite{liu2018tandem}. Conditional
generators and physics-driven global-optimization networks instead search over
multiple candidates~\cite{jiang2019glonet}. Reviews of the field distinguish
direct inverse regression, iterative surrogate optimization, and generative
design, and consistently identify non-uniqueness, data coverage, and physical
validation as central challenges~\cite{so2020inverse,ma2021deep,
wiecha2021deep,jiang2021evaluation}. Ren et al. further show that inverse models
should be compared under a time or candidate budget rather than by one
unqualified output~\cite{ren2020benchmarking}. This motivates our fixed
\(K=128\) candidate pool and equal Top-5 solver budget.

\subsection{Neural Operators and Fourier-Space Inductive Bias}

DeepONet represents a nonlinear operator through branch and trunk networks
~\cite{lu2021deeponet}. FNO instead alternates pointwise transformations with
global convolutions parameterized by learned Fourier modes
~\cite{li2021fno}. A broader operator-learning formulation explains how these
models approximate maps between function spaces and distinguishes graph,
low-rank, and Fourier parameterizations~\cite{kovachki2023neuraloperator}.
Later variants adapt Fourier mixing, geometry handling, or non-uniform data:
AFNO introduces adaptive sparsification and channel structure
~\cite{guibas2022afno}, Geo-FNO learns a deformation to a uniform latent grid
~\cite{li2023geofno}, and NUNO decomposes non-uniform domains into grid-aligned
subdomains~\cite{liu2023nuno}. BISON does not attempt these generalizations. It
uses the stronger prior available in our task---a fixed periodic Cartesian
cell---and specializes the spectral block to two configured mode rectangles and
a joint band-matrix output.

Physics-informed neural operators can add equation residuals during training
~\cite{li2021pino}, while Neural Inverse Operators learn compositions for PDE
inverse problems~\cite{molinaro2023nio}. Our use of ``operator'' is narrower:
BISON is the implemented data-driven map from a permittivity function to a
finite sampled eigenspectrum. It is neither a faithful Maxwell solver nor a
claim that residual minimization alone verifies an inverse design.

\subsection{Learned Electromagnetics and Photonic Band Surrogates}

Learned electromagnetic models span different outputs and evidence types.
MaxwellNet embeds Maxwell-equation residuals in field prediction
~\cite{lim2022maxwellnet}; NeurOLight and PACE develop neural operators for
parametric photonic-device field simulation~\cite{gu2022neurolight,
zhu2024pace}; and a neural-operator surrogate has been used inside free-form
electromagnetic inverse design~\cite{augenstein2023emno}. These studies support
operator learning as a photonic surrogate strategy, but a field solution, a
port response, and an ordered eigenfrequency matrix are different learning
targets.

The closest task-level precedent is the predictive and generative study of
two-dimensional photonic crystals by Christensen et al., which learns complete
band structures and generates band-gap candidates
~\cite{christensen2020predictive}. The recent POD--DeepONet work likewise
studies forward and inverse band design on binary, symmetric two-dimensional
cells~\cite{wang2026poddeeponet}. We do not claim a direct state-of-the-art
comparison because dataset construction, discretization, targets, and replay
protocols differ. BISON's contribution is the combination of a project-native
band-informed FNO, a no-expert latent candidate generator, an independent query
audit, and a controlled same-pool, same-MPB-budget evaluation.
